What an amazing flight!  Top of lift looked similar to yesterday.  Winds were forecast to be a bit similar, but more from the north.  After seeing people getting very high after I landed, game plan was:

* Stay local until about 1pm
* Use the slight tail wind and higher lift to venture out to Les Diablerets (about 15km as the crow flies)
* Launch a tad later, at around 11am.

Top of Lift was about the same as yesterday, around 2400m, but, the thermals were much, much nicer.  Not a washing machine.

I ventured west at around 12:30, a little earlier than planned when I got about 2,500m of altitude.  There wasn’t much over this peak as I was hoping, and I was fighting to stay above the 200m AGL required in this nature zone.  I want to check my track logs, but, I fear I may have briefly breached this.

My initial plan was to go deep and keep high, but, without being able to get very high over the higher terrain, I gave up and went south to have an “out” along the main roads, not as deep.  Yesterday I was getting tossed around here.  Today it was blue, no clouds on the western mountain and 

Found a fantastic climb just where the Thermal triggers in Navigator said I would, following the jump down south!

The rest of the flight I was able to stay high and keep my plan.

I was so impressed to see such a big bird over the lake!  Its wing span must have been larger than my arms outstretched!  Amazing!!

Once I got to Les Diablerets, I did a little tour of the chair lift, got to cloud base there, then decided to push it and see if the thermal markers were going to keep working for me and take me up the back towards the snowy mountains.

Unfortunately, I didn’t find the lift I was hoping for over the local thermal points, so, I decided to figure out where my landing was in town.

The landing spot was smaller than I’d have expected, and, I was worried about the wind blowing through the trees at my time of landing.  As the big soccer field right next door was free, I ended up landing in that to give myself some space away from the possible turbulence of the trees.

Spoke to a local who pointed out the better terrain markers for a northerly wind day to make it back up deep on the other side of the valley.  I should have:
* Taken the hill from the gondola deep back up on a northerly day.
* Crossing towards the “Peak Walk by Tissot”, I could have used the valley wind and worked the ridge a little more.

All and all, not knowing where my landing was exactly, I was still happy to call it early!  Tomorrow is another day!!!
