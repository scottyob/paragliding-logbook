Flight report. 

Had a few moments of stress which I’d like to avoid again for the remainder of this trip.

The plan was to stick with Jason Seto and be his shadow for the day. 

Right after launching I’ve realized my second reserve is not connected perfectly so the anti-shock-load, carabiner locking mechanism wasn’t engaged. Nothing to land for; but things to keep an eye out on. 

We must have launched in a down cycle, as Jason followed the southern spine, I followed the northern spine. Thought process was we couldn’t be crowding one another with light lift and if there’s a big climb we could head over. 

I got super, super low with not much bubbles of lift. Looking back; seto was doing much better, but also scratching, just higher. 

Muuo ended up joining me, but with a little more height. Got a low safe to get us up to cloud base. 

When we were established, Muuo was asking if we should make the head up north. I mentioned I’m not leading without Seto and kept heading south and over launch to try and spot his wing. His radio wasn’t working *sigh* so we were behind from the get go. 

Transitioning north, Muuo took the deep line, I was chasing some clouds more out front. I made the wrong call to join Muuo as I was much higher, but misjudged this as i lost too much height by the time I got there and had to follow down the spine. I was reading myself to land, called to Muuo to go on ahead and had my first lucky low save of the day to climb back up. 

I was then playing catch up as I was alone at the end of the pack. I was proud with my lines and speed to get to the northern waypoint though and get closer to catching up!

My next undoing was pushing a lot of bar heading south to try and catch the rest;  I thought I had the glide with lifty lines to catch up to Muuo and Jess, but I ended up having to work a ridge again to get back up. 

The next stressful moment was Christian told me to head to the next ridge. In hindsight I should have said “no thanks”. Intuition was right, and was in the Lee and fighting to head down around the next spine with how low I was. Glide radio was 2-3:1 over the gully and I was unsure if I’d even have the glide to avoid a tree landing. Take away here is I’m the pilot in command, if I don’t feel comfortable making a low move, don’t. I’d like to avoid this particular stress. 

The rest of the glide out over the flats was with the group. This was great, but I was a bit sad I wasn’t going to get my task. I took one more high turn than the group before making a transition that made all of the difference. I stuck with Jess and Jason who were getting low. 

I *thought* given how high I was and how much it looked liked they were watching that I’d be able to head back; get the launch waypoint and continue on with them. Unfortunately after the launch waypoint, I’d lost sight of both Jess and Jason. 

I did get my next two waypoints, and it was time to heed Dave Oddy’s warnings and give up with the task. The main LZ looked too close to the rain that was by this point forming over the mountains. The big clouds I’d been on the edge of were starting to form quicker than I’d expected. So head to a LZ in town to land on. 

Landing was probably one of the
Most stressful times I’d had paragliding. My initial big LZ was now getting a bit covered over, and unless I made my spirals very very aggressive, I was in lift during my spiral. I tried speed bar and big ears towards the blue sky but was still in lift. I ended up just “out running” the clouds with full speed bar to the blue sky. This worked best as there was no lift out that way; and found a smaller field next to an underground sports area that I spiraled down into. 

A child of no more than 6 was excited to see me land!  I was happy with my landing. I was able to ask in my terrible Spanish “es posible”, walking hand movements and a point to the stadium looking thing. He excitedly said something in Spanish, gave me a over the shoulder universal language “follow me” sign, and I was able to set my wing down and start packing up in the under shelter just before the rain started. 

Packing I had a number of teenagers around who it was hard to converse with but a lot of fun conversations. Was joined with amboise, another I just met. It was fun to try and converse why I didn’t land in the big green field “he good, me, no”. He ended up heading to a wine bar and was joined soon by the rest of my group. 

In hindsight, a great flight but many learnings:
- I am pilot in command, make my own choices to feel comfortable with. 
- Land sooner when clouds start to over develop. It happens sooner than I think.
